Why it matters

Most elections still ask voters, candidates and courts to trust a few operators: who is on the roll, who was turned away, whether a ballot was counted, whether the result matches. ZK Voting turns those claims into things anyone can check.

Private by design

The option you pick is never a readable field in a transaction or a database. Receipts are hashes, so they can't show how you voted.

Publicly verifiable

Registrations, disqualification codes, vote proofs and the final tally are public. An independent auditor can rebuild the result without the operator's private database.

One person, one vote

Every ballot carries a proof that the voter is on the frozen roll and not disqualified. A one-time nullifier rejects any second ballot.

Built to resist coercion

Voters can change their mind privately before submitting, and nothing they are given proves their choice to someone pressuring them. Turnout is published hourly, after the hour closes, so a person can't be matched to a live tick.

Due process for disqualification

Barring someone is a public, coded, on-chain change with a stated reason, not a silent database flag.

No vendor lock-in

AGPL-licensed, run at cost, built to be forked and rebranded. Your group owns the node, the roll and the record.

Who it's for

Any body that needs a roll, a ballot and a result people will accept.

Cities, counties and districts

Run civic elections and referendums with nested districts, measures and articles, and a public results page observers can follow.

Unions and member associations

Hold leadership elections and contract ratification votes where members trust the count without trusting the officers.

Cooperatives and boards

Take board votes and member polls, including weighted and ranked-choice ballots, with an auditable outcome report.

Schools and campuses

Run student government and faculty votes with passkey sign-in and a ballot that's accessible on any device.

Communities and clubs

Quick yes/no, single-choice or multiple-choice polls for any group that wants a private, honest show of hands.

Observers and auditors

Follow registrations, disqualifications and the tally on the public explorer, and verify an outcome report offline.

How it works

Zero-knowledge proofs let the system check facts about a voter without putting the ballot in the clear.

  1. 1

    Register

    Members sign up with a passkey, wallet, email or ID. The roll stores an identity commitment, never an address or document number.

  2. 2

    Freeze the roll

    Officials commit the eligible set and any disqualifications for the election.

  3. 3

    Vote with a proof

    Each ballot proves eligibility and spends a one-time nullifier, so a second ballot is rejected.

  4. 4

    Hourly disclosure

    Vote proofs are published once an hour, after the hour closes.

  5. 5

    Proven tally

    When voting closes, anyone can check that the totals match the accepted ballots.

Run it yourself

Your group runs its own node with Docker Compose, Coolify, Dokploy or CapRover, optionally kept private behind a Tailscale sidecar. Every node serves its self-hosting guides at /docs.

Day-to-day activity lands on the ZK Voting L2, a purpose-built civic sequencer and prover that settles to Ethereum. Final tallies can be mirrored on Ethereum mainnet.

# run your own node
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
curl -fsS http://127.0.0.1:3000/health

Honest status

ZK Voting is under active development. Tests use a mock verifier, the first proving path is in progress and the trusted-setup ceremony hasn't happened yet. Don't use it for a binding election today.

Passing a vote never auto-executes anything. Outcomes are verifiable; acting on them stays with the people.
