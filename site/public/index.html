ZK Voting lets any group run its own elections and polls. The creator chooses a poll or a vote. A poll publishes per-choice counts and turnout on a delayed hourly schedule once 100 ballots are in or the poll ends. A vote publishes turnout on that same schedule, and publishes choices only at the deadline. Zero-knowledge proofs show that only eligible people voted, each one once, and that the published tally matches the ballots. Nobody's choice is ever published.

The big picture

Many civic L2s, one shared record on Ethereum

Anyone can run a civic L2 for their own elections. Each one batches votes and settles them to Ethereum L1, so the record outlives any single operator.

Why it matters

Most elections still ask voters, candidates and courts to trust a few operators: who is on the roll, who was turned away, whether a ballot was counted, whether the result matches. ZK Voting turns those claims into things anyone can check.

Private by design

The option you pick is never a readable field in a transaction or a database. Receipts are hashes, so they can't show how you voted.

Publicly verifiable

Registrations and disqualification codes are public. A poll publishes per-choice counts and turnout on a delayed hourly schedule once 100 ballots are in or the poll ends. A vote publishes turnout on the same delayed hourly schedule. Its choices stay sealed until the deadline. An independent auditor can rebuild a published result without the operator's private database.

One person, one vote

Every ballot carries a proof that the voter is on the frozen roll and not disqualified. In a poll or a vote, a voter can change their choice until the deadline, and the nullifier counts only the last one.

Built to resist coercion

Nothing a voter is given proves their choice to someone pressuring them. Choose a vote when a running choice count would add pressure. Turnout follows the same delayed hourly schedule as a poll. The choices stay encrypted until the deadline, and a voter can replace their vote until then. A poll publishes per-choice counts and turnout on a delayed hourly schedule once 100 ballots are in or the poll ends.

Due process for disqualification

Barring someone is a public, coded, on-chain change with a stated reason, not a silent database flag.

No vendor lock-in

AGPL-licensed, run at cost, built to be forked and rebranded. Your group owns the node, the roll and the record.

How it works

Zero-knowledge proofs let the system check facts about a voter without putting the ballot in the clear. What anyone can see, and when, depends on the kind the creator chose.

Poll

Use a poll when the group wants the picture while people are still responding: a temperature check, a club question, or a non-binding show of hands. Results stay hidden until 100 ballots are in or the scheduled end, whichever comes first. A certified tally also opens that gate. After that, per-choice counts and turnout publish in hourly blocks, each hour 30 minutes after it closes: ballots from 08:00–08:59 appear at 09:30. An hour with fewer than 10 ballots merges forward into a later block. When the scheduled end passes, no more ballots are accepted. The total freezes once the last hour clears the 30-minute delay. The published hourly blocks show the running picture. The certified tally at close is final. A voter can change their vote until the deadline. Only the last vote counts. The zero-knowledge proof and the nullifier enforce one counted vote per voter. At the deadline, one final batch goes to L1. Everyone looking at the dashboard sees the same published counts.

Vote

Use a vote when the choices must stay hidden until the deadline: leadership, a binding decision, or any contest where an early count of choices could change who still turns out. Turnout is visible during the election, published in the same hourly blocks and on the same schedule as a poll. The choices stay sealed. They are encrypted to a public key posted on the L2. The matching private key is split with Shamir secret sharing, so no single party, including the operator, can decrypt early. The choices are decrypted and published only at the deadline. A voter can change their vote until the deadline. Only the last vote counts. The zero-knowledge proof and the nullifier enforce one counted vote per voter. At the deadline, one final batch goes to L1.

    While it is open

    A poll publishes per-choice counts and turnout on a delayed hourly schedule once 100 ballots are in or the poll ends. A vote publishes turnout on that same schedule. Its choices stay sealed until the deadline.

  5. 5

    After the closing date

    No more ballots are accepted. A poll's per-choice counts and turnout freeze once the last hour clears the 30-minute delay. A vote's choices are decrypted and published at the deadline. One final batch goes to L1 for both polls and votes. Anyone can check that the published totals match the accepted ballots.

Run it yourself

Your group runs its own node with Docker Compose, Coolify, Dokploy or CapRover, optionally kept private behind a Tailscale sidecar. Every node serves its self-hosting guides at /docs.

Day-to-day activity lands on the ZK Voting L2, a purpose-built civic sequencer and prover that settles to Ethereum. Final tallies can be mirrored on Ethereum mainnet.

# run your own node
cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d --build
curl -fsS http://127.0.0.1:3000/health

Honest status

ZK Voting is under active development. Tests use a mock verifier, the first proving path is in progress and the trusted-setup ceremony hasn't happened yet. Don't use it for a binding election today.

Passing a vote never auto-executes anything. Outcomes are verifiable; acting on them stays with the people.
